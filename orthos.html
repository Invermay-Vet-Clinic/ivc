Cruciate ligament repair

The knee joint has two cruciate ligaments running though it to keep it stable and aligned when bending to walk, run, and jump. These are the cranial cruciate (CCL or ACL) and the caudal cruciate ligaments.

A very commom injury in dogs is the partial or complete tearing of the cranial cruciate ligament. It can happen when doing regular things like jumping, running, or playing. This rupture, or tearing, causes the knee joint to become unstable and extremely painful. In addition to the ligaments tearing, the cartilage (or meniscus) that cushions the bones can tear at the same time or over time if the ligaments are not repaired. If left untreated, the bones in the joint make contact and form degenerative osteoarthritis, even in young dogs. This is very painful and cannot be reversed.

If you suspect a cruciate ligament injury in your dog, it is best to book in to see us. It is possible that the joint is too painful and the muscles too tense to assess the joint instability. In this case, sedation and xrays may be required.

Surgical repair as soon as possible is recommended for any dog with cruciate ligament injuries. Repair will ensure that further, lasting damage is minimised and the pain is relieved.

We offer two surgical procedures for repairing cruciate injuries. The choice generally depends on the weight of the dog, but there are a number of factors that the vet will discuss and guide you through.

  1. DeAngelis (also known as lateral fabellotibial suture or extracapsular repair) - This is generally a good option for smaller dogs up to 10kg. Prosthetic suture (very strong thread) is used to secure the joint in the same way that the ligament would do if not damaged. In larger dogs, the greater forces exerted on the joint when they move can cause the prosthetic thread to break, but smaller dogs have good results with this surgery.

    Cost: $1700. This includes the surgery, pain medication, 3 post-op check-ups, and comprehensive aftercare guidance.

  2. TPLO (Tibial Plateau Levelling Osteotomy) - This is the gold standard surgery for cruciate ligament repair and involves sawing and repositioning of part of the shin bone, which is then secured with a metal plate to stabilise the joint. Watch this (Warning! Graphic!) video to see what is involved in the TPLO procedure.

    Cost: $4200. This includes the surgery, pain medication, 3 post-op check-ups, post-op xrays, and comprehensive aftercare guidance.

Luxating patella

It may not look like it, but dogs also have knee caps. In some dogs, the knee cap does not stay in it's place, where it normally sitting in a groove in the femur bone. This condition is very common in small and toy breeds and can often be identified by a skip or hop of the back leg when the dog is walking or running. In more severe cases there is lameness, swelling, and pain. Sometimes the dog's leg goes temporarily straight and stiff as the leg is unable to bend when the patella is out of place.

Depending on the grade, or severity, of the luxating patella, treatment can vary from conservative monitoring and physio to surgical correction. If you suspect that your dog has a luxating patella, come and see a vet to evaulate it. If surgery is indicated, we can help with luxating patella surgery. The procedure varies depending on the specific case, but commonly involves deepening the groove where the patella should sit to better align with the dog's knee joint. The cost of the surgery depends on the specifics of the case, and the exact estimate is provided after consultation.

Cost: $1700-2300. This includes the surgery, pain medication, 3 post-op check-ups, and comprehensive aftercare guidance.

Fracture repair

Bone fractures, or breaks, are normally the result of force trauma - a vehicle accident, landing badly in a big jump, from a dog fight, or any number of other accidents that leave your pet in serious pain and at risk of life-long deformities that affect their lives. Fracture repairs are intended to return bones, as much as possible, to their original shape and function. This might involve the use of pins, screws, plates, and other prosthetics to brace the bone while it heals, as well as suturing of tendons and ligaments, or fusing of joints.

By the nature of these injuries, all cases are different and need to be evaluated individually. This typically requires xrays and consultation with the vet about the pet, their lifestyle, life stage, and other factors.

Cost: $1500-$6000. These procedures are not normally offered as a package as with the cruciate and patella surgeries, because the course of recovery is normally unique to the patient and the nature of the injury. Sometimes treatment and rehabilitation can take an extended period of bandaging, xrays, physio, and other types of care as needed.
